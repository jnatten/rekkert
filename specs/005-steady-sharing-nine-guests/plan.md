# Implementation Plan: Steadier, Measurable Sharing for Two Full Courts

**Branch**: `005-steady-sharing-nine-guests` | **Date**: 2026-11-10 | **Spec**: [spec.md](spec.md)

**Input**: Feature specification from `specs/005-steady-sharing-nine-guests/spec.md`

## Summary

The field records show every guest connected over both radios losing its Wi-Fi link about once
a minute, while never losing the host entirely. Reading the code found why that still made
points slow, and why the records could not say how slow:

- **Every link change sets off a full sync round on every link.** Each Wi-Fi drop and return
  sends hellos, the access table and the snapshot to everyone, onto the one Bluetooth queue all
  guests share. That is about 120 rounds an hour per guest (R-3).
- **A guest on both radios sends the host's points back to it** over the other link, which
  doubles Bluetooth traffic per point (R-4).
- **The operating system cuts the Wi-Fi link**, most likely through a TCP keepalive of about
  8 s meeting peer-to-peer Wi-Fi's pauses. The app throws the error away (R-2).
- **Delays are debug-only, subtract two devices' clocks, and are rounded to a tenth.** No link
  knows its radio (R-1).
- **The simulation has no Wi-Fi link, no busy hall and one shared clock**, so it cannot show
  any of this (R-8).

The approach follows Option A's three steps, with the stop the spec puts after the third:

1. **See what happens.**
   - Delays go into every build, measured from the tap to within 0.1 s. Clock offsets come from
     the round trip each point already makes, or the delay is marked unknown (D-1, D-2).
   - Every link entry names its radio, and Wi-Fi drops keep the platform's reason (D-3).
   - Each sharing stint gets a summary that answers "was it on time?" (D-4).
   - Battery readings go into the record (D-12).
2. **Steady the links.**
   - No echo back to the device a point came from (D-5).
   - A link going up or down costs one hello on that link, not a round on all of them (D-6).
   - A looser Wi-Fi keepalive, with a liveness check of the store's own, and a two-day time box
     on finding the cause (D-7).
   - Wi-Fi first with Bluetooth as fallback is held back, unless the measurements ask for it
     (D-8).
3. **Measure, then raise the limit.**
   - The simulation gains a dropping second link, a busy hall, per-device clocks and delays,
     and faster stepping (D-10).
   - The small field run calibrates it (D-11).
   - If nine guests then meet FR-010, `Participants.limit` becomes 9, and "N of M on" shows
     wherever the count does (D-13). If not, the limit stays 7 and the rest ships.
   - The first two-court evening after release confirms it (D-15).

Nothing about how a match merges changes, apart from tap times rounded to the hundredth, which
is weighed below. Everything on the wire is an optional field (D-14). The evidence, decisions
and alternatives are in [research.md](research.md).

## Technical Context

**Language/Version**: Swift 6.2 tools, Swift 6 language mode. Default isolation is
`MainActor` in the apps and in `RekkertSync`; `RekkertCore` is nonisolated.

**Primary Dependencies**: Apple frameworks only, all already in use:

- CoreBluetooth;
- Network.framework;
- WatchConnectivity;
- SwiftUI;
- UIKit's and WatchKit's battery level (`UIDevice`, `WKInterfaceDevice`), read in the app
  shells.

Nothing new.

**Storage**:

- Sharing-record files keep their format, with two optional fields per entry and two new kinds
  (data-model.md).
- `active.json` is unchanged.
- `ClockBook` lives in memory only.
- The calibration fixture is a Swift string in the test target.

**Testing**:

- Swift Testing via `swift test --package-path Packages/RekkertKit`, on macOS with no device.
  - The new suites are listed in [quickstart.md](quickstart.md).
  - The harness gains a dropping second link, a busy hall, per-device clocks and delays, and
    `TestClock.nextDue`.
- `scripts/verify.sh` is the gate.
- The Simulator, via `scripts/share.sh --keep`, for screens and the Wi-Fi path.
- The small field run before release, and the two-court evening after.

**Target Platform**: iPhone on iOS 26, Apple Watch on watchOS 10.0 and later, and macOS 26
for package tests. `WKInterfaceDevice.batteryLevel` exists on watchOS 10.

**Project Type**: Mobile app with an iPhone app, an embedded watchOS app and a widget
extension, generated by Tuist, over the local Swift package `RekkertKit`.

**Performance Goals** (from the spec):

- When nothing is wrong, points reach every device within 1 s for at least 95%, and within
  2 s for all (FR-010).
- Delays are recorded within 0.1 s of the truth, or as unknown (FR-006).
- A target of at most 5 drops per guest per hour in a hall, reported on every run but not a
  release gate (FR-007).
- Back in step within 10 s of a Bluetooth restart on the host (FR-009).
- The guest after the limit is told within 15 s (FR-013).
- The calibrated simulation is within 25% at the 95th percentile (FR-017).

**Constraints**:

- **Principle II.** No change to the reducer, draws, schedules or version vectors. The tap time
  gains one digit, which no rule reads.
- **Additive wire.** Optional fields only. Bluetooth greeting v3 and the TXT record `v = "1"`
  stay. 1.1 keeps working.
- **Watch floor.** Everything on the watch runs on watchOS 10.
- **Shared Bluetooth queue.** The host has one transmit queue for all guests, so any traffic
  saved helps every guest.
- **CI time.** The package job has a 60-minute limit and uses about 8 today. The new long
  suites must fit, or be split across parallel jobs running the same `verify.sh` step (D-10).

**Scale/Scope**:

- A host and up to nine guests, each with a phone and a watch, on two side-by-side courts.
- Each phone may hold two links to the host.
- Evenings of about two hours.

## Constitution Check

*GATE: Must pass before Phase 0 research. Re-check after Phase 1 design.*

| Principle | Gate | Pre-research | Post-design |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Domain logic lives in RekkertCore | Rules stay out of views and platform adapters | **Pass.** | **Pass.** The rules are pure values in `RekkertCore`: `ClockBounds` and `ClockBook`, `SharingSummary`, the stint boundaries, `LinkRadio`, `StandInPolicy.full` and the limit. `MatchStore` and the fan-out call them. The app shells only read the battery level and pass it to the recorder, and render the summary header and "N of M". |
| II. Deterministic, convergent state (NON-NEGOTIABLE) | Every device that has seen the same events computes the same state; reconciliation is monotonic | **Pass**, with one item to weigh. | **Pass.** The merge stays a union by event id. **No echo** (D-5) skips only events the far device provably holds, and the hello still catches up anything missed. **One hello per link change** (D-6) keeps periodic anti-entropy (nudge, retry, idle hello), and the first proof of a device still exchanges access and cards. **The tap time rounded to 0.01 s** (D-1) is written once by the author and copied verbatim, so every device sees the same value, and no reducer, draw or schedule reads the extra digit. Convergence suites run unchanged, and `NoEchoTests` and `LinkChangeTrafficTests` assert convergence alongside traffic. |
| III. Test the engine, test-first | Device-free tests pin every rule; bug fixes start with a failing test | **Pass.** | **Pass.** R-3 and R-4 each become a test that fails on today's code (`LinkChangeTrafficTests`, `NoEchoTests`). FR-006 is proven against simulated clocks with known offsets. Calibration is a test over a committed fixture, not a script. The Wi-Fi link's miss counting reuses the tested `LinkHealth`, with the outcome-to-miss rule pinned beside it. The error-to-code mapping is a pure, tested `DropDetail`. The battery cadence lives in `LinkRecorder`, with its own test. Every FR maps to a suite in [quickstart.md](quickstart.md) (FR-020, SC-008). |
| IV. verify.sh is the gate | Green means `scripts/verify.sh`; silent runtime invariants are asserted | **Pass.** | **Pass.** No new Info.plist key, entitlement or background mode: reading the battery level needs none. The new suites run inside `swift test`, which `verify.sh` calls. If the package step outgrows the CI job, it is split across parallel jobs that each run the same `verify.sh` step on a filter, so `verify.sh` stays the one definition of green (D-10). |
| V. Local-first, private by construction | No servers; nothing leaves the devices at the court unasked | **Pass.** | **Pass.** No new data flow. Clock readings and offsets travel only on the links already between the devices at court. The record's new lines hold seconds, percentages, a radio and a platform error code: no name, score or code. Nothing leaves a phone unless its owner shares the record, as today (FR-004). |

## Risks

| Risk | Effect | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Keepalive is not the cause of the Wi-Fi drops (D-7) | FR-007's drop target is missed | D-3's drop details say so. FR-007 is a target, not a gate, so the release still rests on FR-008 and FR-010, which D-5 and D-6 protect |
| Clock bounds are too wide on slow links (D-2) | Many delays read `unknown`, and FR-010 cannot be shown | Level 4 checks the share of unknown delays. If it is above 10%, sample on the pair link and on direct hellos too, and keep the narrowest |
| Three guests do not load the queue enough to calibrate (D-11) | The nine-guest prediction is weak | The walk-out burst in level 4. The two-court evening checks the prediction (FR-018), and the limit is lowered in the next release if it was wrong (FR-016) |
| The nine-guest suites are too slow for CI (R-8) | The gate takes too long | `TestClock.nextDue`. Otherwise, parallel CI jobs running the same `verify.sh` step |
| The store's real-time timers make timing tests flaky (R-8) | Intermittent failures | Seen during User Story 1, and handled: the store's and fan-out's waits run on the harness's clock at the app's own timings (D-10) |
| A 1.0 guest over Wi-Fi (D-14) | It can sit "catching up" with nothing arriving, as today | Out of scope under FR-015 as clarified: the host shows it as catching up, never in step |
| iOS caps the host's Bluetooth connections below nine guests | Guests refused, or links failing | Only seen on the two-court evening. The record notes refusals, and the limit is lowered in the next release (FR-016) |

**Structure Decision**: the existing layout stays.

- The pure rules (clocks, summaries, radios, the limit) go in `RekkertCore/Sync`.
- Store, fan-out and transport changes go in `RekkertSync`.
- The apps gain a battery reading, a record header and "N of M".
- The simulation's changes stay in the test target.

There is no new package, target, dependency, entitlement or Info.plist key. The one new `Wire`
case is never sent in this release.
