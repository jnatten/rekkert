# Implementation Plan: Name Apple Health Wherever Rekkert Uses It

**Branch**: `002-healthkit-ui-disclosure` | **Date**: 2026-10-27 | **Spec**: [spec.md](spec.md)

**Input**: Feature specification from `specs/002-healthkit-ui-disclosure/spec.md`

## Summary

Build 70 was rejected under guideline 2.5.1 because the reviewer found no sign of Apple Health
in the app. Reading the code shows why:

- **Without a paired watch, nothing mentions Health.** Every Health surface on the phone is
  gated on a paired Apple Watch with Rekkert installed: the Workout section on Home, the
  workout items in a match's menu, and "Workout on your watch" in Settings. An iPhone without
  a watch, and every iPad, shows none of them.
- **Where it is mentioned, it is called "Health".** It is never "Apple Health", and the Apple
  Health icon is nowhere.
- **The watch's Start workout never names Health.**
- **The permission request comes with no lead-in.** It appears on the first press, or when
  the watch app opens.

The approach:

- **An Apple Health section in Settings, shown on every iPhone and iPad.** It sits under
  Apple's own icon, one tap from Home. A short summary says what Rekkert does with Apple
  Health and, when a workout cannot be recorded on this device, why. A row opens a page that
  lists what is saved, what is read, when, why and where it goes, and where in the system's
  settings to change access.
- **One disclosure, in `RekkertCore`.** It holds what is saved and read, why, and the reason a
  device cannot record. The Settings page, the help, the watch's explanation and the types the
  apps ask Apple Health for are all built from it, so the words cannot drift from what is
  collected. Tests pin the list.
- **Apple Health named at every point of use, on both devices.** This covers the Home Workout
  section (icon in its header, never in a button), a match's menu, the watch's menu page and
  idle screen, both "Start a workout?" prompts, the Workouts list and a workout's page, the
  heart-rate chart, the watch's workout page, and failure messages. The words are "Apple
  Health" or "the Apple Health app", never bare "Health".
- **An explanation before the first request**, shown only while Apple Health says a request
  would bring up its sheet. It is one screen that never repeats the sheet's switches.
- **Permission strings reworded to name Apple Health**, and `verify.sh` asserts it.
- **The submission package.** Review notes rewritten around where Apple Health is shown,
  starting with the path that needs no watch, plus a screen-recording script. The icon
  download, the recording and the App Store Connect upload are manual steps for the
  developer.

What the apps read from and save to Apple Health does not change.

The evidence and the alternatives are in [research.md](research.md).

## Technical Context

**Language/Version**: Swift 6.2 tools, Swift 6 language mode. Default isolation is
`MainActor` in the apps and in `RekkertSync`; `RekkertCore` is nonisolated.

**Primary Dependencies**: Apple frameworks only:

- SwiftUI and WatchKit;
- HealthKit (existing), now also its request-status query;
- WatchConnectivity (existing; it already says whether a watch is paired).

**Storage**: None new. Whether Rekkert has asked for access is read from Apple Health each
time, rather than kept in a flag of Rekkert's own (D-5).

**Testing**:

- Swift Testing via `swift test --package-path Packages/RekkertKit`, on macOS, for the
  disclosure, the reason a device cannot record, and the explanation's rule.
- `HelpTests` already holds the help to the screens and to the README.
- `scripts/verify.sh` gains the permission-string assertion.
- Manual walkthroughs on a simulator and on a physical iPhone and Apple Watch, per
  [quickstart.md](quickstart.md). The real-device walkthrough doubles as the screen recording.

**Target Platform**: iOS 26 (iPhone and iPad, one binary) and watchOS 26.

**Project Type**: Mobile app for iPhone and Apple Watch, generated by Tuist, over a shared
Swift package (`Packages/RekkertKit`).

**Performance Goals**: None beyond today's. The request-status query runs only on a press
that would otherwise ask, never while rendering.

**Constraints**:

- Apple's HIG for HealthKit:
  - **Icon**: only Apple's artwork, with the name "Apple Health" beside it and clear space of
    a tenth of its height. Never a button, never inside text, never masked or tinted.
  - **Wording**: "Apple Health" or "the Apple Health app", never "HealthKit".
  - **Access**: managed only through the system's settings, with no in-app switches over what
    flows.
  - **Permission screen**: no custom screen that repeats the system's.
- The icon cannot be fetched by the build. It comes from Apple Design Resources under Apple's
  terms and is added to both asset catalogs by hand.
- Help items are at most 80 characters, and every SF Symbol drawn must be explained or waived
  (`HelpTests`).
- Watch text must fit the smallest supported watch at the largest text size.
- `001-fix-session-sharing` changes `HomeView`, `MatchOptionsMenu`, `RootView`,
  `SettingsView`, `HelpCatalog`, `README.md`, `Shared/AppModel.swift`, `WatchCourtPage`,
  `WatchMenuView` and `WatchDemoLaunch` in parallel. The edits here are kept small and local
  so a rebase over it is mechanical.

**Scale/Scope**: About 15 screens across two apps; one new phone page and one new
explanation screen per device; no new data, no wire change.
